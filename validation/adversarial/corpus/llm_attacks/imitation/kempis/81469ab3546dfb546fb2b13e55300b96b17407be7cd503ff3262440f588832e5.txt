Many there are who would do great things for God, and yet despise the small things which He layeth before them day by day. They think upon the deserts of the holy fathers, and upon watchings and fastings beyond measure, and meanwhile they neglect the little duty of the hour, they murmur at a slight word, and they cannot keep silence when silence is required of them. Such a man buildeth upon the air, for he would begin at the top, and hath laid no foundation. He that is faithful in that which is least, saith the Lord, is faithful also in much.(1)

2. Think not, therefore, that the way of perfection lieth in rare and wonderful works, which are seen of men and commended by them. It lieth rather in this, that thou do well and with a pure intent whatsoever thou hast to do, though it be of no account in the eyes of any. To draw water or to labour in the garden for the love of God is more pleasing unto Him than to dispute subtly for the love of praise. For God looketh not so much to the greatness of the work as to the love wherewith it is done; and a little thing done with a whole heart outweigheth a great thing done with a divided one.

3. Oh, how many occasions of virtue pass by us in a single day, and we perceive them not! A brother speaketh hastily, and thou mightest bear it in patience; a hard task is laid upon thee, and thou mightest take it up without complaining; some small pleasure is offered, and thou mightest deny it to thyself for Christ’s sake. These are the daily crosses which the Lord giveth to His servants, that they may be proved in little before they are proved in much. But we let them go by, and wait for some great trial; and when it cometh we are found unready, because we have not been exercised in the lesser.

4. Be not slothful, then, in small matters, nor say within thyself, “This is a light thing, and it mattereth not.” For the soul is not lost all at once, but by slow degrees; and he who maketh light of one negligence will soon make light of many. A small leak, unless it be stopped, sinketh the ship; and a little spark, if it be not quenched, consumeth the whole house. Watch therefore over thy words, thy looks, thy thoughts, and the manner of thy going out and coming in, for in these things is thy heart either kept or lost.

5. Neither be thou cast down if thou find thyself often failing even in these little things. It is the nature of man to fall, and the work of grace to raise him up again. Humble thyself, and begin anew, and reckon not that thou hast done anything until thou hast persevered unto the end. He who each day striveth a little, and each day falleth a little, and each day riseth again with a good will, shall at the last be found further upon the way than he who began with great fervour and afterwards grew weary.

6. Lord, Thou knowest how weak I am, and how quickly I am turned aside by the least matter. Give me grace to be faithful in the little which Thou committest unto me, to do my common duties with a willing heart, and to seek Thy glory in the meanest of my labours. Let me not despise the day of small things,(2) nor look for any reward save Thee alone; for if Thou be with me in little things, I shall not lack Thee in great. Amen.

(1) Luke xvi. 10. (2) Zechariah iv. 10.
