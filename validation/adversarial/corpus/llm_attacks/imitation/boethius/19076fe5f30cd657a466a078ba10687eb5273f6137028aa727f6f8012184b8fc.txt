Then said I: 'Thou hast shown me that the gifts of Fortune are none of mine, and I am content to let them go. Yet one thing still rankles in my breast, and I will not hide it from thee. When I think upon those who brought me to this pass, my heart burns within me, and I can scarce forbear to pray that the same measure may be meted out to them. Is it, then, forbidden me even to desire that justice should overtake them?'

'Nay,' said she, 'I forbid thee nothing which reason allows. But consider with me awhile what manner of thing this is which thou desirest. Thou wouldst have them suffer. Tell me, then, dost thou think them happy at this present?'

'They seem so,' said I; 'for they have their will, and sit in the places from which I have been thrust.'

'Thou sayest "they seem," and rightly; for seeming is the whole of their felicity. Hast thou forgotten what we agreed but lately--that nothing is good which can be torn away, and nothing truly thine which another may give or take? Every one of these things in which thy accusers now exult is of this kind. They hold them as a man holds water in the hollow of his hand, and the tighter he grips, the faster it runs out between his fingers. But there is more. That which they have gained, they have gained by fraud and perjury; and so they have bartered away the one possession which no tyrant could have taken from them, and have received in exchange a thing which every tyrant may take. What dost thou think of such a bargain?'

'That it is a fool's bargain,' said I.

'Then wouldst thou not rather pity a fool than hate him? Or art thou wroth with the sick man because his fever burns him? Believe me, child, wickedness is the sickness of the soul, even as fever is of the body; and he who is sick in the soul carries his punishment within him, though he wear purple and be followed by a throng of clients. For what can be more wretched than to be the slave of one's own lusts, to dread the discovery of one's crimes, to lie wakeful in the night lest some other informer should do to him what he hath done to thee? These are the tormentors that already wait upon thine enemies, and they need no help of thine.'

'Yet I would fain see it,' said I.

'There speaks the wound, and not the reason. Mark how this desire of vengeance, which thou deemest a love of justice, would make thee in turn the bondsman of the very men thou hatest. For so long as thy peace hangs upon their ruin, thou hast given them a lordship over thy mind which they could never have won by all their accusations. Dost thou remember how Socrates, when he stood before his judges, declared that Anytus and Meletus might indeed slay him, but could not harm him, since it is not permitted that the worse should hurt the better? He bore them no anger, nor sought any evil for them, but left them to the god's judgment. And herein was he more free in his fetters than they upon their benches.

'Wherefore, if thou desirest that justice be done, leave it to Him who alone judges without error, and who knows the hidden seasons of every recompense. Some He punishes swiftly, that others may take warning; some He suffers to go on awhile, that they may have space for amendment, or that by their very success they may make their downfall the more manifest. But do thou keep thine own soul within its citadel, and suffer not the dregs of another's wickedness to trouble the clear waters of thy mind. So shalt thou be avenged in the noblest way, for thou wilt be what they can never be--at peace.'
