# Proctor Script — one page, read it before you walk in

*This is for the person running the room, not the operator. Nothing here
requires you to know how Original works — just what to say and what to look
at. Keep this page open on your own laptop during the exam.*

## Before the exam starts

Say to the class:

> "Phones face-down on the desk, please. Laptops open to the exam page only —
> close everything else. This exam is monitored for authorship, not graded on
> how you type; the monitoring is explained in the disclosure you already
> received."

Then, walking the room:

- [ ] Confirm every visible phone is face-down. A phone in a bag or pocket is
  fine — face-down on the desk is the visible signal you're checking for.
- [ ] Confirm every laptop is on the exam page and nothing else (no second
  monitor, no second tab visible).
- [ ] Glance at your seat map note if you have one — it's for you, not the
  students; use it to remember who's who when a tile needs a name later.

**(Available from Bluebook vX — QR phone-park ritual):** once this ships, add
here: display the QR code on the projector, ask students to scan it with the
face-down phone, type their name or initials when prompted, then set the
phone back down face-down. This turns "phone is present" into a signal you can
watch on your own screen instead of walking the room the whole period. Until
this ships, the face-down instruction above is the whole ritual.

## During the exam

Keep your own screen open to the proctor tile view. Each student gets a tile.

- **Green tile ("parked")** — the phone is present and idle. No action.
- **Amber tile ("dropped")** — the phone went quiet (screen off, app closed,
  out of range) or was picked up. Walk over, quietly, and check in with that
  student. Don't announce it to the room.

Two rules, no exceptions:

1. **One warning per event.** If a student's tile flags once, a quiet word is
   enough — you don't need to hover after that unless it flags again.
2. **Never accuse from a tile alone.** A tile is a signal that something
   changed on the phone, nothing more. It is not evidence of cheating and it
   is not what decides anything. The authorship analysis — the writing itself,
   compared against that student's own history — is the evidence. A tile just
   tells you where to look.

If something feels wrong beyond a tile (a student you can see is using a
phone, a laptop with a second window open), handle it the way you'd handle
any other exam-conduct issue — the tiles don't change that process, they just
give you one more thing to notice.

## After the exam

- [ ] Confirm every student submitted before they leave. If the room's roster
  view shows someone missing, check with them before they walk out — it's much
  easier to fix in the room than after.
- [ ] If anything happened worth remembering — a dropped tile that turned into
  a real conversation, a student who had a technical problem, anything you'd
  want to recall in a month — write one line in `PILOT_LOG.md`. Keep it
  boring: date, who, what happened, what you did about it.
