# Typing-Biometrics Normal Ranges — Research Brief (2026-08-13)

## What we asked
What published, citable normal ranges exist for keystroke-dynamics measurements in **adults composing free text** (not transcription), relevant to Original's six Tier 17 behavioral features: `typing_speed_cv`, `burst_ratio`, `deletion_rate`, `pause_density`, `paste_event_rate`, `revision_depth`? Sources: keystroke-logging writing-process research (Inputlog/ScriptLog), keystroke-dynamics biometrics, educational writing-process studies.

## What the literature says

- **Typing speed / IKI variability (`typing_speed_cv`, bounds 0.0–2.0).** Best direct number: coefficient of variation of inter-keystroke intervals in **free composition** ≈ **0.99 ± 0.19** (13,000 sessions, Stony Brook corpus; transcription of essays was nearly identical at ≈0.99 ± 0.12) — reported in a 2026 keystroke-forgery study ([arXiv 2601.17280](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17280v1)). Automated/scripted injection sat at 0.05–0.27, i.e. human CV is ~6–7× higher. Context anchors (transcription, 168k volunteers): mean **51.6 WPM (SD 20.2)**, mean IKI **239 ms** (fastest typists ~122 ms) ([Dhakal et al., CHI 2018](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3173574.3174220)). Free composition is much slower: the classic Karat et al. (1999) figure is **~19 WPM composing vs ~32.5 WPM transcribing** for average users ([Words per minute, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Words_per_minute)); both figures predate today's higher baseline typing skill. Implication: a real composing CV near 1.0 sits at the midpoint of our (0, 2) bounds — reasonable.

- **Burst ratio (fraction of keystrokes with IKI < 150 ms, bounds 0.0–1.0).** **No published normal range found** for this exact proportion. The writing literature measures bursts differently — words produced between ≥2 s pauses ("P-bursts"): adults average **~9 words per burst**, with more-skilled writers ~4 words longer than less-skilled ([Kaufer, Hayes & Flower 1986, via Springer 2024 replication context](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11145-024-10621-9)). The arXiv forgery paper uses the same <150 ms burst definition as ours but reports no population baseline. Sanity logic only: with mean IKI ~239 ms in fluent transcription, a substantial minority-to-half of keystrokes under 150 ms is plausible for fluent typists; near 0 and near 1 are both suspicious.

- **Deletion rate (`deletion_rate`, bounds 0.0–0.5).** Transcription: error corrections (Backspace/Delete) average **6.3% of keystrokes (SD 4.5)**; trained typists 5.9% vs untrained 6.5% ([Dhakal et al. 2018](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3173574.3174220)). Fatigue pushes it up modestly (4.2%→5.4% over a 2-h session; [Frontiers in Psychology 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01113/full)). Free composition adds revision deletions on top of typo corrections, so composing rates run higher and are far more variable; in large writing-assessment logs, insertions + deletions together are ~99% of all actions and **lower deletion proportion correlates with higher writing scores** ([Zhu et al. 2019, ETS/Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ets2.12247) — abstract-level; full text paywalled). A composing norm of roughly **5–30% of keystrokes** is defensible from these sources; our 0.5 ceiling comfortably covers it.

- **Pause density (long pauses per 100 words; repo threshold >3 s, bounds 0.0–20.0).** **No published per-100-words rate found**, and note our 3 s threshold differs from the field standard: writing-process research canonically uses **≥2 s** to separate cognitive pauses from motoric inter-key gaps ([Wengelin, pause-threshold development, diva-portal PDF](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:834468/FULLTEXT01.pdf)). Indirect anchors: undergraduates composing familiar-topic essays had 71% of *sentence-initial* IKIs under 1 s ([Medimorec & Risko 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11145-017-9723-7)), and P-bursts of ~9 words between ≥2 s pauses imply on the order of **~10 two-second pauses per 100 words** for a typical adult — fewer at the stricter 3 s cut. Our 0–20 bounds bracket that plausibly; pause proportion of total time is a standard Inputlog output but published values are heavily task-dependent.

- **Paste event rate (`paste_event_rate`, bounds 0.0–5.0 per 100 words).** **No published normal range found.** Assessment-context studies typically disable copy-paste entirely and treat pasted/transcribed text as the anomaly class to detect (99% detection of transcribed vs authentic essays from process features; [Crossley et al., EDM 2024](https://educationaldatamining.org/edm2024/proceedings/2024.EDM-short-papers.47/)). The honest prior for proctored first-draft composition is **~0**, matching the comment already on the bound; any nonzero rate is signal, not noise.

- **Revision depth (repo definition: mean chars per deletion event, bounds 0.0–50.0).** **No published range for chars-per-deletion-event found.** The field's closest measure is the **product/process ratio** (final chars ÷ total chars produced): worked examples in the Inputlog methods literature show 0.86 for a light reviser vs 0.64 for a heavy one, i.e. **~14–36% of produced characters discarded** in ordinary academic composing ([Chan 2017, Language Testing in Asia](https://languagetestingasia.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40468-017-0040-5); [Leijten & Van Waes, Inputlog methods](https://www.inputlog.net/wp-content/uploads/2020_CJSLW-Designing_KSL-studies.pdf)). Most deletions are short (single-word typo fixes), so a low mean chars-per-event with a heavy tail of bulk deletes is the expected shape; the 50-char ceiling and ">30 = bulk rewriting" comment are consistent with that but uncalibrated.

## How this should inform Original's Tier 17 bounds
These findings support sanity-checking the existing `NORM_BOUNDS` in `original/constants.py`, not changing them: the strongest numbers (IKI CV ≈ 1.0, deletion ≈ 6% transcription / up to ~30% composing) all fall comfortably inside the current bounds, and nothing here contradicts them. Most published figures come from transcription tasks or research-instrumented sessions, not a production capture pipeline like Bbook's, and per-student normalization means population ranges bound plausibility rather than define profiles. Tier 17 should stay in `DISABLED_FEATURE_GROUPS` until live keystroke data lets us calibrate against our own capture — also note our 3 s pause threshold deviates from the literature's 2 s standard, which is worth a deliberate decision when that calibration happens.
