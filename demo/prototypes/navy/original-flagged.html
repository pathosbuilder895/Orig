What the analysis found in plain English

This paper reads differently from James's usual voice in 19 of 97 writing habits. In how his sentences look, 6 habits look different: his letter patterns are unusually predictable, and he uses dashes far more than usual. In how his ideas connect, 7 habits look different: linking phrases (however, therefore) appear more often than usual, and his sentences start in unusually varied ways. In how he makes his case, 6 habits look different: he softens claims (perhaps, it seems) more than usual, and asks more rhetorical questions than usual. Together they point to the same thing: prose that is smoother and more uniform than James writes.

Having the conversation a suggested approach
Worth doing
  • Open with the work, not the flag: ask James to walk you through his argument in ¶5: how he got from Cyril to the anathemas.
  • Show one side-by-side and ask what he notices about the two voices.
  • Ask about his process: drafts, tools, timeline, where he wrote it.
  • Offer a path back: a supervised rewrite of one section in Bluebook, which you can approve as reference writing if it helps.
Worth avoiding
  • Don't present the numbers as proof: they describe distance from his own habits, nothing more.
  • Don't open with an accusation; it forecloses the conversation the evidence is meant to open.
  • Don't rely on memory in a dispute: this packet and the case reference are the record.
