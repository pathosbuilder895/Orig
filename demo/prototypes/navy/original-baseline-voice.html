How this student writes
A careful, first-person voice
that thinks out loud in long sentences.
Across three teacher-approved papers, James writes the way he talks through a problem: patiently, in his own person, weighing one side against the other before he lands.
Long sentences Thinks out loud Speaks as “I” Weighs both sides Active voice
The short versionplain English, no jargon

James writes in long, unhurried sentences and isn't afraid to let one run: he builds a thought clause by clause rather than in short bursts. When he makes a point he tends to state it plainly and then soften it: a confident claim followed by a “perhaps” or an “it seems,” as if he's testing the idea aloud.

He writes in the first person (comfortable saying “I think” or “we see”) and prefers the active voice (“Paul argues,” not “it is argued”). He likes to set ideas against each other, reaching often for however and but, and he leans on the vocabulary of his field when the moment calls for it. None of this is unusual for him: it's simply his normal, and that's exactly what the baseline records.
